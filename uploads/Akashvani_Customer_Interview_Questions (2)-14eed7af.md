# Akashvani Customer Interview Questions

Use these questions for interviews with people who have recently faced a serious relationship, marriage, separation or divorce decision.

## 1. Customer background

1. Tell me briefly about your age, location, work and relationship status.
2. What major relationship decision have you faced recently?
3. When did this situation happen?
4. What triggered the need to make a decision?

## 2. Understand the real problem

5. Tell me exactly what happened.
6. What decision were you trying to make?
7. Why was that decision difficult?
8. What were you most worried about?
9. What could have happened if you made the wrong decision?
10. How long did you remain confused or delay the decision?
11. How did the situation affect your emotions, work, health or family life?
12. On a scale of 1 to 10, how painful was the problem? Why?

## 3. Understand current behaviour

13. What did you actually do to find an answer?
14. Who did you speak to first?
15. Did you consult any of these?

    - Friends or family
    - Therapist or counsellor
    - Astrologer
    - Relationship coach
    - Lawyer
    - Spiritual practitioner
    - Online content or AI
    - Nobody

16. Which option helped the most?
17. What was disappointing or missing?
18. How many different people or services did you try?
19. How much time did you spend trying to solve the problem?
20. How much money did you spend?
21. What stopped you from getting better help?

## 4. Understand the desired outcome

22. What kind of answer or support were you looking for?
23. Did you want someone to advise you, validate your thinking or help you decide?
24. What would a successful outcome have looked like?
25. How quickly did you need help?
26. Would one session have been enough, or would you have needed follow-ups?
27. What information would an expert need to understand your situation properly?

## 5. Test trust

28. What makes you trust someone with a private relationship problem?
29. What qualifications or experience would matter to you?
30. Would you trust one expert, or would you prefer a second expert to review the advice?
31. What would immediately make you distrust the service?
32. How important are privacy and confidentiality?
33. Would you be comfortable sharing birth details, relationship history and personal documents?
34. What proof would you need before booking?

## 6. Test the Akashvani concept

Only explain Akashvani after understanding the customer’s past behaviour.

Say:

> Akashvani helps people navigate important life decisions. A customer shares their situation, speaks with a suitable expert, receives a second expert’s perspective and gets a practical written guidance note with follow-ups.

Then ask:

35. What is your honest first reaction?
36. Which part sounds most valuable?
37. Which part sounds unclear or unnecessary?
38. How is this different from what you already use?
39. What would stop you from using it?
40. What would Akashvani need to prove before you trusted it?
41. Would astrology make the service more useful, less credible or make no difference? Why?
42. Would you prefer astrology-led guidance, practical counselling or a combination of both?

## 7. Test willingness to pay

43. What did you pay for the alternatives you previously tried?
44. What result would make this service worth paying for?
45. What price would feel suspiciously cheap?
46. What price would feel reasonable?
47. At what price would you need strong proof before purchasing?
48. If the complete service cost $159, what would make you reject it?
49. Would a refundable $25 deposit make starting easier? Why or why not?
50. If this service were available today, would you book it now?

If they say yes, ask:

51. Would you be willing to pay the deposit or join the pilot today?

A real payment or deposit is stronger evidence than a verbal yes.

## 8. Close the interview

52. What should I have asked but did not?
53. Who else do you know who has recently faced a similar decision?
54. May we contact you for a paid pilot or follow-up interview?

## Interviewer rules

1. Ask about a real recent experience, not an imaginary future situation.
2. Do not explain or defend Akashvani during the interview.
3. Ask “Why?” and “What happened next?” whenever an answer is vague.
4. Record exact customer words.
5. Separate what customers say from what they have actually done.
6. Do not count politeness, compliments or “sounds interesting” as demand.
7. Strong evidence means previous spending, urgent action, a deposit, a booking or a referral.

## Key takeaways to capture after every interview

1. The exact painful problem.
2. How frequently it occurs.
3. What the customer currently does.
4. What they have already spent.
5. What is missing from current alternatives.
6. Their strongest reason to choose Akashvani.
7. Their biggest reason to reject it.
8. Evidence of willingness to pay.
9. Required trust signals.
10. The next assumption Akashvani must test.
